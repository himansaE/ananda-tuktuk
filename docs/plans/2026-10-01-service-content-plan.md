# Ananda's tuk-tuk service: content and visitor journey plan

Date: 1 October 2026

Status: proposed content strategy, grounded in the current project and the owner's brief. This document does not change the website. Business details identified for confirmation must be checked with Ananda before being published as promises.

Owner clarification: short rides, longer outings with several stops, hotel pickup, waiting at attractions, and return to the hotel are all available. The language should be friendly and simple, with photos and easy visual choices carrying much of the explanation.

Design requirement: design for phones first. The motion must meet a high agency craft standard: deliberate choreography, immediate interaction feedback, coherent timing, clean transitions and smooth behavior on real devices. Preserve the friendly voice and current visual identity.

## 1. Purpose and audience

The website is Ananda's digital introduction and service brochure. Its main visitors are tourists who scan a visiting card supplied by their hotel, or scan a QR code that Ananda shows on his phone. It should help him explain his service even when a conversation is short or language is a barrier.

The site must answer these questions quickly:

1. Who is Ananda, and what does he do?
2. Where does he drive?
3. Can I arrange an outing that includes several places?
4. What else can I visit besides the famous sights?
5. What is included, what costs extra, and how do I ask for a price?

Keep the existing visual identity. Improve the wording, order of information, and enquiry flow. The content should work for someone reading simple English on a phone outside a hotel or beside the tuk-tuk.

### Established by the brief

- Ananda is a tuk-tuk driver who takes tourists to different destinations.
- Guests can arrange a short ride or a longer outing with several stops.
- Ananda offers hotel pickup, waiting at attractions, and return to the hotel. These services are confirmed and should be explained confidently.
- The service also introduces visitors to restaurants, spas, antique and heritage shops, woodwork, and herbal shops.
- Hotel cards and Ananda's phone are important entry points.
- The current appearance is broadly satisfactory; the main problem is clarity and completeness of content.
- The owner wants a friendly, simple voice and a strongly visual presentation.
- Mobile is the primary layout and interaction target; desktop builds on that foundation.
- High agency motion quality is a requirement, with the detailed direction and assessment criteria in section 16.

### Planning assumptions

- Dambulla, Sigiriya, and Embul Ambe remain the core area, as represented in the current site.
- WhatsApp is the main enquiry channel, supported by phone calls.
- Exact pickup coverage, waiting charges, fare inclusions and any fixed half-day/full-day products still need details. The availability of pickup, waiting and hotel return is already confirmed.
- Visiting a business is an optional choice made by the guest. Ananda confirms the route and any arrangements in conversation.

## 2. Recommended direction

Position the business as **a local tuk-tuk driver who helps visitors explore Dambulla, Sigiriya, and nearby places**.

Use two separate ideas throughout the site:

- **The ride:** how much time the guest has, hotel pickup, chosen stops, waiting while they explore, and the return to their hotel.
- **The places:** landmarks, village scenery, restaurants, crafts, shops, and wellness venues.

This explains why a guest might choose Ananda for an afternoon of local discoveries as well as transport to Sigiriya. Describe him as a driver and local companion. Only advertise guided visits inside attractions or professional guide credentials if verified.

### Approaches considered

| Approach | Advantage | Trade-off | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service-first homepage, with fuller details on existing pages | Every QR visitor immediately understands the business; one main introduction to maintain | Needs some homepage reordering and new practical content | Recommended |
| Separate short QR landing page | Can provide a very brief introduction | Duplicates content and creates another page to maintain | Consider only if testing shows the homepage remains too long |
| Fixed package catalogue | Makes comparison easy if Ananda has established products and prices | Can make a flexible driver service appear rigid and imply unconfirmed inclusions | Use example outings initially; add packages only when they are real offers |

## 3. First-screen content

The first phone screen should show the service, location, Ananda's name, and a useful action. Place the text and actions before the large image on mobile. The current hero puts its image before the text and has no direct explanatory paragraph.

### Proposed hero copy

**Headline:**

> Tuk-tuk rides around Dambulla & Sigiriya

**Introduction:**

> Hi, I'm Ananda! I'll pick you up from your hotel, take you to the places you choose, wait while you explore, and bring you back. One stop or a few. It's up to you.

**Primary action:** Chat on WhatsApp

**Secondary action:** See where we can go

**Supporting location line:** Based in Embul Ambe, near Dambulla.

Use a real photograph of Ananda with his tuk-tuk when available. The service explanation must remain visible without waiting for a slideshow or animation. Keep rotating destination photography as supporting atmosphere if it still works with the new text.

Avoid relying on phrases such as "Crafting journeys," "Field notes," or "Hidden gems" to explain the business. Warm, personal language can follow the practical explanation.

### Friendly voice and visual presentation

Use Ananda's first-person voice: "I pick you up," "I wait for you," and "I bring you back." Keep each sentence short and use everyday words. The public site should use "places," "stops," "ride," "food" and "shops" in preference to planning terms such as "itinerary," "transport arrangements" or "experience catalogue."

Show three clear service facts with small icons close to the hero:

- **Hotel pickup** — "I'll meet you at your hotel."
- **Time to explore** — "I wait while you enjoy your stops."
- **Hotel return** — "I'll bring you back when we're done."

These describe available services, not a promise that waiting or return is free. Explain the agreed fare and any waiting charges in the practical details.

Use photos as the main way to discover places. Each place needs a clear name, one short sentence and a visible action. Let guests tap for practical details or add a stop. Keep selected stops visually marked and show a small "Your stops" summary so it is easy to see their choices.

Example photo-card copy:

| Photo subject | Short caption | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sigiriya | See the famous rock fortress. | Add this stop |
| Local restaurant | Stop for a Sri Lankan lunch. | Add this stop |
| Woodwork shop | Have a look at local woodwork and crafts. | Add this stop |
| Spa | Take some time to relax. | Add this stop |
| Herbal shop | Browse local herbs and spices. | Add this stop |

Use real, relevant photos. If an individual venue has not been confirmed, present the category as an interest and use "Ask me about this" rather than implying a booked or guaranteed venue. Put visit times, venue charges and reservation details behind an easy-to-open details area; keep the category overview light.

Preserve the current visual style. Use a few useful interactions for browsing and choosing stops, with important information immediately readable. On a phone, fit the short service introduction and a clear action near the photo instead of showing a tall image before the explanation.

## 5. Rides and experience categories

### Ride options

| Visitor need | Suggested label | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach one place | A short ride | "Need to get somewhere? Tell me your hotel and where you'd like to go." Offer a return when requested. |
| Explore for a few hours | A few stops together | "Pick a few places you like. I'll drive, wait while you explore, and bring you back." |
| Spend longer exploring | A day with Ananda | Build an outing around available time; publish half-day/full-day labels only if offered |
| Unsure where to start | Help me choose | Tell Ananda your interests and time available; ask for suggestions |

Airport transfers, long-distance transport and multi-day trips should appear only if Ananda confirms those services. Their presence in the current copy is not evidence of availability.

### Places and experiences

| Category | Examples to describe | What the content must explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Famous sights | Sigiriya, Dambulla Cave Temple, Pidurangala | What the visitor does; Ananda's transport role; ticket and any separate guide arrangements |
| Village life & scenery | Village roads, lake viewpoints, countryside photo stops | Which actual locations he visits; whether activities beyond transport are included or arranged separately |
| Sri Lankan food | Local restaurants, rice and curry, cafes | Type of food, confirmed venue, and whether a reservation is needed; meals priced separately unless included in an agreed offer |
| Crafts & woodwork | Woodcarving workshops, craft shops, makers | Distinguish watching a demonstration, joining a paid activity, and browsing a shop |
| Heritage & antique shops | Traditional crafts, heritage shops, antique browsing | Describe the actual shop accurately; avoid guarantees about authenticity or value |
| Spas & wellness | Spa visits, massage or Ayurveda venues | Ananda provides transport; the venue provides the service; confirm appointment needs and venue charges |
| Herbs & spices | Herbal shops, spice gardens, local products | Distinguish a shop visit from a garden demonstration; describe the visit without treatment claims |
| Wildlife outings | Minneriya or Kaudulla access/meeting points | Verify whether he provides only transfer or also arranges a separate safari operator; identify what the quoted price covers |

These categories follow the owner's brief. Sri Lanka Tourism also distinguishes dining, spa/wellness, spice gardens, tourist shops and arts/crafts, which supports presenting them as visitor interests. It does not establish that a particular venue is nearby or that Ananda offers it. [Official tourism activity categories](https://www.srilanka.travel/).

### Information required for each place

- Plain name and category.
- One sentence about what the guest can do there.
- Real venue/location and accurate photo.
- Approximate visit duration, if checked; avoid unsupported travel-time promises.
- Ananda's role: transport, waiting, local introduction, or a separately arranged activity.
- Booking needs and costs outside the transport quote.
- Whether the stop is optional.
- Visible action: **Ask me about this** for an unconfirmed venue, or **Add this stop** for a published place.

For local businesses, name only venues Ananda actually knows and visits. Initially, a category can say "Ask Ananda about local woodcarving shops" if individual venues have not been confirmed. Do not invent a directory.

Suggested guest-choice copy, subject to confirming his policy:

> Choose the stops that interest you. Shopping and spa visits are optional, and you decide whether to buy anything or book a service.

Explain any relevant referral arrangement before a guest agrees to a recommended business stop. The content should help the visitor understand the recommendation and make their own choice.

## 6. Example outings

These are planning ideas, not fixed packages or promises of duration, access, availability or price. Confirm geographic practicality with Ananda before publication.

1. **A sight and a Sri Lankan lunch:** choose Sigiriya or Dambulla Cave Temple, then add an optional restaurant stop.
2. **An afternoon of local discoveries:** choose a woodcarving or heritage shop, an herbal/spice stop, and a cafe or countryside photo stop.
3. **A relaxed outing:** choose a spa appointment and optional food or scenery stops around it.

Show the main activity first and label the add-ons as optional. Avoid filling every hour or combining distant places merely to create an impressive list.

Suggested section introduction:

> Start with a place you want to visit, then add the stops that interest you. These ideas can be changed around your time and preferences.

The example's action should open the planner with the idea selected. It should not make the guest reconstruct the choice from an empty form.

## 8. Enquiry and WhatsApp flow

Browsing and asking a question should be easy. A guest can contact Ananda immediately or use the optional planner for a more specific request.

The planner should accept:

- Ride interest: short ride, a few hours, a day, or help choosing; only show confirmed products.
- Hotel name or pickup area, with free text for an exact location.
- Date and preferred time, when known; include an "I'm flexible" option.
- Number of guests and relevant luggage information.
- Selected sights/experiences, plus an optional custom request.
- Return/waiting preference, with hotel return available and timing agreed in conversation.

Avoid making every field compulsory for an initial enquiry. Do not require an email address or account.

Use friendly field labels such as "Where are you staying?", "When would you like to go?", "How many people?" and "What would you like to see?" Show the main choices visually and keep extra notes optional.

Use one clear final action: **Ask for a price**. Opening WhatsApp creates an enquiry; it does not reserve a ride or send a message automatically. Suggested note: "Send me your idea. We'll agree the time and price in our chat."

### Example message generated from a completed request

> Hi Ananda! I'd like to plan a ride with you.
>
> Date/time: 12 October, around 9 am
> Pickup: our hotel in Dambulla; I'll send the exact location
> Guests: 2
> Interests: Dambulla Cave Temple, woodcarving, Sri Lankan lunch
> Return: back to our hotel, please
>
> Can you help us plan the stops and let us know the price? Please also tell us about any tickets or other costs. Thank you!

The date and details above are example input, not an actual booking. In the real message, include the guest's selections. If they have not chosen any stops, ask for recommendations rather than treating it as an error.

Keep a phone number and a copyable itinerary available for guests who cannot use WhatsApp. The existing contact form currently prevents submission without sending anything; replace that form with a working WhatsApp enquiry flow unless there is a concrete need for another channel.

## 9. Practical information and trust

Use clear answers to these questions, based on Ananda's actual operating terms:

The first three services are already confirmed. Suggested short answers:

- **Can you pick me up?** "Yes! Tell me your hotel, and we'll arrange a time."
- **Will you wait for me?** "Yes. I wait while you explore your stops. We'll agree the timing when we plan the ride."
- **Can you bring me back?** "Yes! I can bring you back to your hotel after your outing."

Keep the overview answers short. Explain the covered pickup areas and any waiting/return charges in the fare details.

- Can you pick me up from my hotel, and in which areas?
- Will you wait while I visit a place? How is waiting charged?
- Can you bring me back, and how do we agree the return time?
- How many passengers and how much luggage can you carry?
- Is the quoted fare per ride, per tuk-tuk or per person?
- Are tickets, meals, safari vehicles, spa services or purchases included?
- Can I change my stops? Could that change the price?
- Do I need to book a spa, restaurant or activity separately?
- What languages do you speak?
- How do I confirm or cancel my ride?

If prices vary, publish the basis of the quote and ask for a price. If Ananda supplies fixed prices, show currency, unit, starting/pickup area, duration, inclusions and exclusions. Keep those terms consistent across every page.

Real photos and genuine reviews should provide trust. Confirm the identity of people labelled as Ananda in existing photos, the permission to use guest photos, and the source of every review. Remove unverified ratings, rider totals and experience statistics from public copy.

## 10. QR and mobile use

- Design and review the phone layout first at 360 and 390 CSS pixels wide. Check 320 and 430 as well, then tablet and desktop. Desktop receives a wider composition rather than determining the phone's content order.
- Use a vertical page with generous photography, brief captions and a clear action. Category chips may scroll horizontally through native touch scrolling; keep the places themselves easy to browse vertically.
- At normal text size on a 360 by 740 viewport, show the service, location and primary action in the first screen. Let the layout grow naturally at larger text sizes and shorter heights; do not clip content to force it into a viewport.
- Start with 16px side gutters on the narrowest screens and 20px on standard phones. Use a 16px body and input text baseline, comfortable line spacing, and concise headings that wrap naturally.
- Set custom buttons and icon controls to at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, with clear spacing. This adopts the enhanced touch target recommendation; it is not a claim that the whole site conforms to WCAG AAA. [W3C target-size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html).
- Keep the main action reachable near the bottom of the phone when needed. Use one contextual action area: chat initially, then the selected-stop summary and price enquiry when guests start planning. Reserve space so it never covers content; hide it while an overlapping panel is open and keep focused inputs clear of it when the keyboard appears.
- Account for display safe areas in the persistent action and detail panel. [MDN safe-area environment variables](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/env).
- Point hotel-card and phone QR codes to the same stable homepage on the final owned domain.
- Print Ananda's name, a service description, phone number and a readable URL beside the card QR.
- Make the first-screen text useful without further explanation from the hotel or driver.
- Give Ananda a dedicated QR image he can keep on his phone once the final URL is known.
- Check the printed card and phone QR on real devices before distribution.
- Prefer static, readable essentials over animation-dependent content; avoid making a visitor watch a long visual sequence to find services or prices.
- Use short paragraphs, meaningful headings and large tap targets; show important actions without requiring a hover.
- Use photos, clear category choices and short captions to explain the outing. Longer practical details should open when the guest wants them.
- Keep English simple initially. Add carefully reviewed translations later if guest language patterns justify them.

A useful phone-sharing target is that someone can understand the service in roughly 10 seconds, identify a relevant outing within 30 seconds, and start an enquiry without navigating through several pages. These are design targets to test, not measured current performance.

## 11. Search and sharing content

The priority is helping the visitor who already arrived from a QR code. Google's guidance also favors content with a clear audience and purpose, useful first-hand information, and answers that help the reader complete their goal. [People-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

Use a distinct title and description for each page. Include the business, service and local area naturally; use clear headings and descriptive photo text. Suggested home title: **Ananda's Tuk-Tuk Rides | Dambulla & Sigiriya**. Suggested description: **Explore Dambulla and Sigiriya with Ananda, a local tuk-tuk driver. Discover sights, Sri Lankan food, crafts, shops and spa visits. Ask about your ride.** Add a canonical homepage URL, sitemap, robots configuration and a useful sharing image once the final domain is known. [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

Consider business structured data only after the identity, contact details and publishable address are verified. Google's local-business rich result requirements include a name and address. If a suitable public address is unavailable, do not fabricate one or promise rich result eligibility; select markup supported by the real facts. Do not add self-serving rating markup. [Local-business structured data requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business).

## 16. Mobile-first motion and craft specification

### Direction

Keep the feeling of an inviting travel website with a real driver behind it. The signature moment is the opening photograph settling into the existing arch shape while the short headline takes its place. After that, motion mainly helps guests browse, open details and see their selected stops.

Use the existing GSAP, ScrollTrigger and `@gsap/react` packages for coordinated sequences. Use CSS transitions for simple press and selection feedback. Relevant design engineering, animation, GSAP React, timeline, ScrollTrigger, performance and review skills are already available; no skill or motion-library installation is needed for this plan.

### Choreography and interaction timings

These are starting design values to implement and assess visually. They are not evidence of completed animation quality.

| Moment | Purpose | Planned movement and timing | Phone behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First arrival | Introduce Ananda and establish the visual character | Photo settles from scale 1.03 to 1 over 600ms inside a static arch crop; heading settles by 8px over 300ms. Coordinate in one timeline with a 50ms offset. | Body copy and chat action are visible and usable from first render; no loading curtain or wait for the entrance to finish. |
| First view of an experience group | Make the photos feel composed | A light 8px settle over 300ms; 50ms stagger among a maximum of three visible items. Play once. | Names, captions and controls remain readable and usable; quickly scrolled-past content is left in its final state. |
| Press a button or add a stop | Confirm the tap | Scale to 0.97 over 100ms on press, return over 160ms; selected badge/check appears over 200ms. | Selection state updates immediately; rapid tap/untap does not queue effects. |
| Change category | Explain the change in visible places | 200ms opacity transition; keep stable photo aspect ratios and preserve selected stops. | Native horizontal scrolling for category chips; no forced page movement. |
| Open a place's details | Keep context while revealing information | A bottom detail panel enters with a 250ms transform, closes over 200ms; backdrop fades over 200ms. Reuse the chosen photo. | Clear name, brief description, costs/arrangements and an Add this stop action. Panel scrolls naturally and has a visible close control. |
| Update Your stops | Show what changed | Badge/summary content changes with a 200ms transition. | The number and selected places reflect the state immediately; enquiry stays available. |
| Open the mobile menu | Show navigation without delaying it | Panel enters over 250ms and exits over 200ms. Four links settle over 150ms with 30ms offsets, within the panel's opening time. | Labels are tappable throughout; focus returns to the menu button on close. |
| Open a practical answer | Reveal the explanation | A local 200ms accordion transition; animate only this small region and measure its layout cost. | A short answer remains easy to read; respect reduced motion and rapid open/close. |

### Detail panel behavior

Use accessible dialog behavior, including focus containment, Escape/close support and focus restoration to the place that opened it. Guests must be able to read details and close the panel with ordinary taps and scrolling. Preserve all selected stops when opening and closing it. No drag gesture is required to complete the journey.

Treat interrupted transitions as normal: opening a second place, closing midway, changing categories rapidly, tapping a selected stop again, and leaving the page must finish in the requested state. No delayed callback may reopen an old panel, undo a selection or update a removed component.

For keyboard-initiated menu, category and selection actions, update immediately rather than playing the touch movement. Focus movement and selected-state announcements must never wait for an animation.

### Scrolling and performance

- Preserve native touch scrolling. Do not add artificial touch inertia, phone scroll pinning or vertical scrolling that moves content sideways. The existing ride journey already uses a vertical compact layout; retain that model.
- Desktop wheel smoothing and mild photo parallax are enhancements only for wide screens with fine pointers and no reduced-motion preference. They must not delay access to content.
- Keep the service headline fixed in meaning. Replace competing auto-rotating words and the continuous phone ticker with readable service information and guest-controlled browsing.
- Prefer transforms and opacity. Keep the arch crop static while its photo transforms; avoid large animated blurs or masks across the phone screen.
- Use temporary layer promotion only where measured to help. Pause off-screen decorative effects and stop unnecessary work while the browser tab is hidden.
- Give photos stable aspect ratios and responsive sizes. Load the main photo early and defer lower-page images; do not eagerly download every slideshow layer before the guest starts browsing.
- Scope GSAP setup to each component, clean it up on navigation, and ensure callbacks and timers cannot keep animating removed elements. [GSAP React cleanup guidance](https://gsap.com/resources/React/).
- Set up mobile, desktop and motion-preference variants with appropriate cleanup when conditions change. [GSAP responsive animation guidance](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.matchMedia()/).
- With reduced motion, remove travel, parallax and large scaling; use immediate states or brief opacity/color feedback. Never hide useful content because an animation was skipped. [MDN reduced-motion guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion).
